Chapter 01 — A story, not a sales pitch

Ramesh opens his shutter at 6 AM.
The day that used to defeat him.

Pune, Kothrud. Ramesh Yadav's kirana is eleven feet wide and forty years old — inherited from his father, along with a cloth-bound bahi-khata and a ballpoint pen that only works if you press hard. By 9 AM the pen has already failed him twice.

The morning rush doesn't wait for paperwork. A schoolboy wants two biscuits. Aunty wants atta on udhaar. A delivery boy drops a crate of oil and thrusts a printed bill at him. Somewhere in the middle, a customer flashes a phone screen — “payment ho gaya, dekho” — and Ramesh has four seconds to decide whether to trust a screenshot he's never seen before.

This is the part the software industry never designed for. Every accounting app assumed Ramesh would sit down, type, pick tax slabs, reconcile. Ramesh never sits down. His hands smell of haldi. His internet dies every time it rains. His English is the English of signboards, and his accountant speaks in a language of forms that cost ₹500 a visit to translate.

So the bahi-khata stayed. And the money scattered — some in the notebook, some in GPay notifications, some in PhonePe, some as screenshots, some as memory. At Diwali, when the bank asked for “audited statements” for a ₹2 lakh loan, Ramesh had a cloth book with smudged pages. The loan died there.

“Mujhe English software nahi chahiye. Mujhe koi chahiye jo meri sun sake.”— Ramesh, and roughly 12 million kirana owners like him

KhataCopilot started from that sentence. Not as an app — as a counter companion. One device, no internet needed for the core, three jobs: log every rupee by voice or camera, unify money scattered across UPI apps and cash, and verify payments before trust. Everything else — GST exports, ONDC catalog, the loud “₹200 received” announcement, the thermal bill — is just what falls out naturally once those three are true.

What follows is Ramesh's Tuesday — hour by hour — with the companion on the counter. Then the honest technical details, the downloads, and the fine print we refuse to hide.

Why this story matters

  • 80%+ local merchants still run on paper or memory for daily books.
  • 3 UPI apps minimum per counter — collections fracture across streams.
  • Fake payment screenshots are the fastest-growing counter fraud.
  • Zero audited books = zero working-capital loans for most MSMEs.
Sources: MSME Ministry headcount (63M enterprises), on-ground merchant interviews in Pune, 2025–26. Figures are directional, not a census.

Chapter 06 — The fine print, up front

What stays on your phone.
What we will never claim.

🔒 Your books stay home

Ledger, parties and invoices live in on-device SQLite. Nothing uploads unless you switch on cloud sync yourself. Voice audio is transcribed and dropped; receipt photos are read for totals, not warehoused.

⚖️ Not a CA, lender or GSP

GST figures are drafts in the portal's format — your CA files, not us. Any future credit comes from a named RBI-regulated lender; the app only facilitates. We never initiate or move money.

🗑 Deletion that means it

Profile → Delete Account wipes the on-device ledger, with a web request page for the cloud copy. If a build ever claims deletion and fails silently, treat it as a P0 bug and tell us.

Privacy Policy·Terms of Service·Delete my data
